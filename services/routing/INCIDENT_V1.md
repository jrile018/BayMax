# Incident evidence bridge v1

`services.routing.incident` binds one frozen incident evidence snapshot to one validated physical trip checkpoint. Its result is evidence context for a view. The v1 operational gate stays closed. It cannot establish safe roads, safe destinations, resource availability, dispatch eligibility, or calculated outcomes.

## Public API

`verify_frozen_contract(manifest, contract_bytes, receipt_bytes) -> True` requires the original manifest as bytes and pins its SHA-256 (`f7d8fbd0613ba34f188e77e43a96219703914ccfe8e1e3e49b929585858d8ede`), version, reviewed commit, immutable/capability flags, three contract file SHA-256 hashes, and independent review receipt SHA-256. `contract_bytes` maps the three exact relative contract file names to their bytes. The caller reads these bytes from its trusted artifact store. The bridge itself uses no artifact path, current working directory, root contract import, or `sys.path` mutation.

`bind_checkpoint(graph, checkpoint, epoch_utc, graph_identity) -> dict` restores the complete real `TripExecutor` checkpoint with `TripExecutor.from_checkpoint`, then rechecks its canonical bytes. `graph_identity` is an explicit dict with exactly `graph_fingerprint`, `graph_version`, `metric_crs`, `origin_lonlat`, and `source_metadata`, all matching the supplied directed graph. The returned dict has `cutoff_utc`, `ledger_watermark`, and `in_flight_commitments`. The watermark binds full executor and ledger checksums, both audit lengths, `now_s`, epoch, graph fingerprint/version, and source/metric identity. Commitments are sorted by dispatch ID and include each full actual assignment and trip record in every noncompleted phase, including loading, traveling, servicing, and stranded. The returned JSON is a defensive copy. Supply its latter two fields to `EvidenceLog.snapshot(...)` in the trusted incident ingestion layer.

`bridge_incident_v1(*, graph, checkpoint, epoch_utc, graph_identity, snapshot, trusted_expected_snapshot_hash, edge_bindings, manifest, contract_bytes, receipt_bytes, horizon_minutes) -> IncidentBridgeResult` verifies the frozen dependency and binds the snapshot's watermark and commitments *exactly* to a fresh checkpoint restoration. The caller must obtain `trusted_expected_snapshot_hash` independently from a trusted `EvidenceLog` issuance; the snapshot's internal SHA-256 checks integrity but cannot authenticate its origin. Both hashes must match. The snapshot must have the frozen v1 structure, current accepted records and source references derived from its history, a cutoff equal to checkpoint time, and no known accepted/fetched/reviewed data after that cutoff. Forecast validity times may extend into the future; they do not become known evidence before acquisition. The requested horizon is 1–10080 minutes after the exact checkpoint cutoff.

`edge_bindings` is a separate list of typed evidence-context dictionaries, each with exactly `source_id`, `item_id`, `revision`, `raw_sha256`, `edge_ids`, `issued_at`, and `received_at`. Its source identity must be a current accepted closure record, and the edge IDs must be nonempty, unique IDs in the supplied directed graph. `issued_at` denotes the source claim's issue time; when the record has a published time, issue cannot precede it. `received_at` denotes receipt of the accepted mapping envelope and must be no earlier than the referenced revision's fetch and acceptance. Issue and receipt must satisfy issue ≤ receipt ≤ cutoff. A lead, superseded or retracted record, or foreign source reference cannot bind an edge. These bindings only preserve provenance. The bridge does not read `edge_ids`, `open`, `closed`, `safe`, `stock`, or capacity from free-form record payloads and does not turn the typed bindings into closures or road coverage.

Every input timestamp uses frozen v1's strict UTC `Z` grammar with at most six fractional digits. Checkpoint `now_s` must yield an exact whole-microsecond offset from `epoch_utc`; finer values are rejected instead of rounded. Equivalent valid timestamp spellings compare by instant. A `SafetySnapshot` in the result has empty `covered_edges`, empty `safe_nodes`, no closures, and provenance that says incident v1 is disabled. Its version is only a context marker. `can_calculate_operational_outcomes` is always `False`; all operational categories are unknown and unverified even if records are present. No executor method that changes state is called.

The caller remains responsible for authenticating source bytes, reviewers, and the expected snapshot hash, and for obtaining the graph/checkpoint from its authoritative store. Version 1 does not validate semantic truth or completeness of operational claims. Separate reviewed contracts are required before any route or outcome capability may be opened.

The regression suite includes `fixtures/routing/frozen_incident_v1/input.json`, a test-only byte fixture generated on the cloud from the immutable frozen manifest and three pinned contract files plus independent review receipt. It includes original manifest bytes and records their pinned SHA-256. Tests verify all decoded bytes through `verify_frozen_contract`, then run an actual frozen `EvidenceLog` snapshot in an isolated test module. Production code neither imports nor executes those fixture bytes.
