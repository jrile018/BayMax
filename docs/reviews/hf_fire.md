## Findings

1. **HIGH — an unrelated completed delivery can become a new need.** `services/intake/schema.py:151-165` checks that quantity and unit spans lie inside the model-selected claim evidence, but never restricts that evidence to one clause or checks that the count belongs to the request. `services/intake/review.py:72-86` emits the resulting command after acceptance. Pinned repro text: `10 bottles were delivered to Pine School. Water is needed at Pine School.` A proposed `water` claim with the entire 73-character report as its evidence, quantity `10` and unit `bottles` from the completed delivery, and location `Pine School` passes with no flags. A **scripted** `review(...,'accepted','scripted-reviewer')` emits `propose_need(category='water',quantity=10,unit='bottles')`. This does not demonstrate a real human approval or simulator mutation; it demonstrates that exact character spans alone do not enforce the required source-to-need relation. Reject claim evidence spanning multiple clauses and bind each quantity/unit to the request clause, including completed-event adversarial tests. Keep authenticated human acceptance as a separate integration gate.

2. **HIGH — a mode-restricted road can emit unrestricted `open`.** `services/intake/schema.py:120-125` marks qualified access only for `pedestrians`, `on foot`, and `peatones`; `schema.py:174-179` accepts other qualifiers, and `review.py:77-86` emits `propose_access_observation(status='open')`. Pinned repro `Highway A is open to bicycles only.` with exact whole-sentence evidence and a mapped edge has no `qualified_access` flag; scripted acceptance emits an open observation without vehicle mode. If applied to all vehicles, this could incorrectly reopen the route for an ambulance or truck. Represent eligible modes as a reviewed typed fact, or abstain on `only`, `except`, bicycle, vehicle-class, and time-limited qualifications until a human records a supported scoped observation. The downstream router must never interpret an unscoped open as universal access.

3. **MEDIUM — the headline exact-report/evidence metrics credit oversized evidence.** `evals/intake/run.py:35-40` excludes all evidence spans from `signature`; `run.py:71-89` only verifies that quotes match *some* source slice; `run.py:202-208` calls matching signatures an exact report. Changing WF01's evacuation evidence from its specific clause to the **entire report**, with every label/slot unchanged, scores `exact_report=true`, `valid_schema=1/1`, and `evidence_quote_support=7/7` in the pinned repro. These metrics do not establish that evidence supports each claim. Score evidence span/clause alignment separately and avoid describing current exact-report results as evidence accuracy.

4. **MEDIUM integration guard — forecast issue time is not reconciled with observed-source availability.** `services/hazards/wildfire/adapter.py:125-143` checks `observed_at_utc <= retrieved_at_utc`, and `adapter.py:257-309` accepts any `issued_time_s` while copying source IDs into edge forecasts. An observed source stamped 01:00 and retrieved 01:01 after a 00:00 scenario epoch was accepted by `simulate` and cited on a forecast issued at scenario second 0. This is a metadata/time gate gap for a caller that uses same-day incident observations. The exercise's assumed weather and spread fields are not explicitly bound to that ledger item, so this probe does **not** prove that the physical model consumed future observations. Root should keep source timestamps and forecast issue time in the snapshot and exclude any observation unavailable at that decision; the hazard adapter should reject or explicitly label future-source entries when it publishes an issued forecast.
